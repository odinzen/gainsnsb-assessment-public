## Composition sections

- Gerdes: sections AB-Sn with x_Ga = x_Sb (or x_In = x_Sb). The printed x_Sn is an ATOM fraction. Gerdes
  Eqs. (12)-(14) define x_A = x_B = (1-x)/2 and x_C = x, and Eq. (18) only reduces correctly on that basis.
  So x_Ga = x_Sb = (1 - x_Sn)/2. A "mol% GaSb" basis would be different. The formula-unit fraction
  X_GaSb = (1 - x_Sn)/(1 + x_Sn) is given in a column for comparison only.
- Katayama: rays from the Ga corner, Ga_x(Sb_y Sn_1-y)_1-x with y = 0.75, 0.50, 0.25 and
  x_Ga = 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9. x_Sb = (1-x_Ga) y, x_Sn = (1-x_Ga)(1-y). None of these alloys lies on
  the GaSb-Sn section (x_Ga = x_Sb would need x_Ga = y/(1+y), not measured). The two datasets do not overlap.

## Converting the emf to a chemical potential

Cell: (-) W, Ga(l), Ga2O3 | ZrO2(Y2O3) | Ga-Sb-Sn(l), Ga2O3 (+) W. Both electrodes carry Ga2O3, so the
virtual cell reaction is Ga(l, pure) -> Ga(in alloy), with n = 3 electrons per Ga (Ga2O3 + 6e- = 2 Ga + 3 O2-).

    mu_Ga(alloy) - G_Ga(liq, T) = -3 F E          (paper Eq. 1; E in volts, F = 96485.33 C/mol)
    a_Ga = exp(-3 F E / (R T))
    mu_Ga^xs = -3 F E - R T ln x_Ga = R T ln gamma_Ga

E is positive in every row, so a_Ga < 1. With E/mV = a + b T:
mu_Ga - G_Ga(liq) = -3F(a + bT)/1000 J/mol, partial enthalpy dH_Ga = -3F a/1000 and partial entropy
dS_Ga = +3F b/1000. The reference state is pure liquid Ga at the same T (Ga melts at 302.9 K). In a
pycalphad check, MU(GA) is referred to SER, so subtract G of pure liquid Ga at T (or set the reference
state to LIQUID) before comparing.

Recomputing the printed table: a_Ga from the printed E(1073 K) reproduces every printed a_Ga within
0.2 %, gamma = a_Ga/x_Ga and alpha = ln gamma/(1-x_Ga)^2 reproduce exactly. So the convention above is
the one the authors used.

## Things to flag

1. **Gerdes GaSb-Sn, Table 1 and Fig. 1 disagree.** At the same x_Sn the Fig. 1 symbols lie 11-58 K
   above Table 1 for x_Sn >= 0.10 (35 K at x_Sn = 0.5, 58 K at 0.7). The pattern is a one-row offset.
   If each figure symbol is paired with the table T of the row above it (fig 0.10 with table 0.06, fig
   0.15 with table 0.10, ..., fig 0.90 with table 0.85), the 12 pairs agree with RMS 2.8 K. Fig. 1 has
   14 liquidus symbols against 16 table rows: no symbol at 0.05 or 0.06, and no symbol anywhere near the
   table's last value (607 K). The authors' text (p. 287) says their regular-solution curve lies above
   the experimental liquidus by about 10 K at intermediate x_Sn. In Fig. 1 the upper dashed curve is about
   12 K above the symbol at x_Sn = 0.6 (803 vs 791.5 K), which fits the figure. It would be 51 K above the table
   value. This points to a misaligned x column in Table 1, but it is not proven. **Do not fit or score
   against Table 1 GaSb-Sn x_Sn >= 0.06 until this is resolved.** The digitized Fig. 1 symbols are
   internally consistent with the paper's own model comparison. A second source for the GaSb-Sn
   liquidus would settle it.
2. **Gerdes InSb-Sn** agrees with Fig. 2 within +/-5 K from x_Sn = 0.15 to 0.85, except x_Sn = 0.80
   (table 546 K, figure 560 K). The table's 0.75 and 0.80 rows are nearly equal (547, 546 K) on a steep
   part of the curve, and Fig. 2 has no symbol at 0.75. Treat those two rows as doubtful. Below
   x_Sn = 0.15 the figure has symbols at 0.05 (794 K) and 0.10 (782 K), while the table has
   0.02/0.04/0.06/0.10 at 790/783/779/777 K. They do not pair one-to-one.
3. **Eutectic temperatures.** Gerdes print no invariant values. From the figures:
   GaSb-Sn drawn line 505.1 K, halt symbols 507.5 +/- 1.6 K (n = 11), liquidus reaching Sn at
   x_Sn of about 1 (degenerate). InSb-Sn drawn line 509.9 K, halt symbols 508.7 +/- 3.1 K (n = 13), and
   they fall into two groups (about 511 K and about 505 K). The drawn liquidus meets the line at
   x_Sn = 0.88. A eutectic of L + InSb + (Sn) at 510 K lies 5 K above the Sn melting point (505.08 K). That
   is possible only if dissolved Sb raises the (Sn) solidus more than In lowers it, so check it against
   the model rather than assume it. The pure-Sn halt symbols plot at 504.7 and 506.2 K, so the
   temperature scale is good to about 1 K at this end.
4. **Gerdes method details missing.** Heating or cooling DTA is not stated. The authors attribute
   liquidus scatter to easy undercooling (p. 288). That suggests some halts are cooling arrests, which
   bias liquidus low. No uncertainty is given.
5. **Katayama, y = 0.25, x_Ga = 0.3.** The printed fit E = -25.38 + 0.0548 T gives 33.42 mV at 1073 K.
   The printed E(1073) is 33.21 +/- 0.18 mV, and the printed a_Ga = 0.340 follows from 33.21. All other
   rows agree within 0.02 mV. One of a, b or E is misprinted. The a_Ga difference is 0.6 % (0.338 vs 0.340).
6. **Katayama E(T) slopes are weakly determined.** Each alloy covers about 100 K. The implied partial
   enthalpies of Ga jump non-monotonically along a section (y = 0.75: +1.3, -2.8, +8.6 kJ/mol at
   x_Ga = 0.1, 0.3, 0.5). Use mu_Ga at 1073 K (or E at mid-range) as the primary datum. Do not fit dH or
   dS from these slopes. Do not extrapolate E(T) outside each alloy's span (T_min_K, T_max_K, read from
   Fig. 2 at +/-3 K). The spans run from about 1027 to 1165 K, wider than the stated 1050-1150 K.
7. **Katayama Fig. 2 raw points, Figs. 3-5** (isoactivity lines, activity curves, ternary G^xs from Darken
   and Chou) were not digitized. Figs. 3-5 are derived or interpolated, not measurements. Fig. 5's caption
   prints the unit as kJ/(mol K), which is a typo for kJ/mol.
8. **Katayama Table 2** units are not printed (J/mol is implied). The Sn-Ga pair is written i = Sn,
   j = Ga, so odd-order terms change sign for a Ga-before-Sn TDB. The text cites both refs [1] and [11]
   for Sb-Sn. These fits come from the authors' binary data and cover 1000-1200 K only.
9. Katayama assume all alloys are liquid throughout. Check that the model puts each alloy above its
   liquidus at its T_min_K. The Sb-rich, Ga-poor alloys (y = 0.75) are the ones to check first (not verified here).
10. The Gerdes PDF text layer has OCR errors in Table 1 (797 read as 191, 777 as 177, 547 as 541). All
    values here were read from the page image.
